2. LOOK
====================================================================
Page, margins, colours and fonts are exactly the bulletin's (Bulletin_V1_Rules_Structure
section 2): A4 portrait, white; navy #09132A, electric blue #3FA9F5, blue ink #1B75BA,
blue tint #E8F4FE, amber #FFC45A and amber tint #FFF3DB, greys #566074, #8A93A3,
#D8DEE7, #EEF1F5; Source Serif 4 for headlines and reading text, Instrument Sans for
navigation, labels and source lines; every font embedded.

What differs in the report (points, size/leading):
- Product title "Full Report": Serif Display 50/54, with 15 mm under the masthead rule
  (the bulletin uses 11 mm).
- Section title (category name): Serif Display 27/31 (bulletin 25/29).
- Story headline: Serif Subhead 14/17.2 (bulletin 16.5/20.5).
- Story text: Serif 9.6/13.9, 5.5 pt after each paragraph (bulletin 10.8/16).
- Source line: Sans 8.4 in the bulletin, Sans 7.8/10.5 in the report.
- Label row: 7 pt after it (bulletin 9).

Front page, top to bottom: masthead "AI NEWS DESK" with its blue bar, navy rule, title,
date, purpose line (indented 30 mm from the right), coverage line, 13 mm space, then
CONTENTS: blue ink label, "n categories · n stories", navy rule with a 28 mm blue accent,
and one linked row per category. In the report each category row also shows that
category's first (highest ranked) headline in Serif Italic 9.4 under the name, cut to
fit one line. Then one row for each extra section, marked "Feature". The front page
ends there: the first category starts on page 2.

Every page: the same footer and running header as the bulletin, reading "Full Report"
instead of "Daily Bulletin". The PDF opens with its bookmarks panel showing the
categories.

Section header: the bulletin's (navy 0.8 pt rule with a 28 mm blue accent, "SECTION nn",
category name, "n stories · ranked by importance"). A section starts on the same page
as the previous one when its heading and the opening of its first story fit; otherwise
it moves to the next page. A heading never sits alone at the bottom of a page.

Story grid (report only): under the full-width section header the stories run in TWO
columns with an 8 mm gutter and a hairline down the gutter. Column 1 fills first, then
column 2; on the last page of a section the two columns are balanced. Stories in the
same column are separated by a hairline. There is no hanging rank numeral in the report.

Story block, order:
1. Headline.
2. Label row: "Importance: HIGH (8)", "Rank n of m in category", status pill.
3. The paragraphs (section 5.3).
4. Source line: "Source: <Publisher> ↗ · domain.com", the publisher name a live link to
   the original article.
The headline, label row and first lines of text never split across columns or pages,
and a source line never starts a column alone.

Extra sections (The Bigger Picture, About this edition): full-width section header,
then the text in the same two columns (a full-width line at 9.6 pt would run to about
120 characters).

If the last page would be nearly empty (under a quarter full), the program retries with
tighter vertical spacing and keeps it only if it saves a page.

====================================================================
4. SELECTION
====================================================================
1. Source: the day's verified pool after quality checks. Items re-dated into the window
   (an old event reported again without new facts) and duplicates of the same story are
   held back and listed in the day's QA record, not printed.
2. Rule (Rafael, 29 Sep 2026, CLAUDE.md rule 14): the report is score 5 and up plus
   the Fun Side by default. It is sorted by category, so a reader can go to
   their own section, where a 5 may matter to them. Stay flexible on unusual days: if
   5 and up passes about 150 stories, raise the cutoff; if it gives very few (under
   about 50), lower it. State the cutoff and the count; never stop to ask about them.
   Examples: 27 Sep 2026 printed every kept story, 138; 28 Sep 2026 used 5 and up, 78;
   29 Sep 2026 used 5 and up, 127.
   The Fun Side (Rafael, 30 Sep 2026): 5 to 10 stories, leaving out repeats of earlier
   days; its top 3 also go in the Daily Bulletin.
3. Pool target (Rafael, 28 Sep 2026): about 15 candidates per category, Fun Side about
   10. Counts are guidelines, not gates.
4. Score bands: 10 CRITICAL, 8 and 9 HIGH, 6 and 7 MEDIUM, 1 to 5 WATCHLIST. A famous
   name or a big dollar figure never raises a score.

====================================================================
5. WRITING
====================================================================
5.1 Reader and phrasing: use the shared rules and the Full Report section of
    Article_phrasing_instructions_AIND_V1; that file alone defines the reader level.
5.2 Every story is written from the full source article when it can be opened. Keep
    every hedge and every attribution ("Bloomberg reports"). A proposal is not an
    action, a company claim is not an independent finding, a possibility is never
    written as a fact. Stories are written in our own words, not copied, and always
    credit and link the source (section 6).
5.3 Story format (LV1.1 section 8, same as the bulletin):
    - Headline: one full sentence saying what happened, not leading with an unexplained
      acronym or product name.
    - Importance label and score, rank, status (shown in the label row).
    - Paragraph 1, the summary: one or two complete sentences; the first is the event
      with its attribution, the next gives the practical "why it matters" or new facts.
    - Paragraph 2: "Why it matters: " plus one sentence naming what changed, who it
      affects (people first, then institutions, then companies) and the honest doubt.
    - Paragraph 3, only when the original article could not be opened: "Note: we could
      not open the original article; this entry relies on its headline and related
      coverage."
5.4 No sales language, no buy or sell advice, no marketing words. Name a stock or
    sector only when the market effect is the news.
5.5 The Bigger Picture (LV1.1 section 9):
    - Title "The Bigger Picture — Daily AI Analysis"; first line "What today's
      developments could mean for technology, business and everyday life".
    - Built from today's report plus the previous 7 to 14 reports.
    - Fixed order: 1. What AI can do now; 2. People and society; 3. Safety, security and
      law; 4. Infrastructure; 5. Markets (at most a quarter of the analysis, no tickers,
      no buy or sell advice).
    - Every claim labelled: Fact, Inference, scenario, speculation, weak signal,
      unknown or missing data. Cite the story by item number. A Fact must be attributed
      to its source as in the story.
    - Forecasts: each gives a probability, a horizon (days to 4 weeks, 1 to 3 months,
      3 to 12 months, 1 to 3 years, 3 to 10+ years), a confidence level and its signal,
      what would confirm it and what would weaken it, and who is affected (people before
      companies). Mutually exclusive scenarios add up to about 100 percent.
    - Required ending: A. Highest conviction developments to watch; B. Low probability,
      high impact scenarios; C. Signals that would change this view; D. Key unknowns
      and data gaps.
    - It is the desk's analysis, kept separate from reported facts.

====================================================================
6. CREDIT AND LINKS
====================================================================
1. Every story names its publisher and links to the original article, never to a
   Google News or other redirect link, and never with tracking codes. Credit to the
   people who reported the story is required; this is why the link stays even on
   headline-only stories.
2. The source name is the publisher only. Internal notes ("via", "(blocked)", research
   remarks) are removed before printing; a long name is cut at its first comma.

====================================================================
7. FIXED TEXT
====================================================================
- Title: Full Report. Date: "<day> <Month> <year>", for example 27 September 2026.
- Purpose line: "The day's comprehensive AI intelligence report: every story we kept
  from today's pool, grouped by subject and ranked by importance."
- Coverage line: "<reporting window> (<hours> hours) · <n> stories · <n> categories".
- About this edition, three short paragraphs: where the stories were collected and for
  which window; how many full articles were read and how many are headline only; the
  score scale and the line "Nothing here is investment advice."

====================================================================
8. INPUT FORMAT (what build_pdf.py reads)
====================================================================
The same format as the bulletin (Bulletin_V1_Rules_Structure section 8):
{"title": "Full Report", "date": "...", "purpose": "...", "coverage": "...",
 "categories": [{"name": "...", "items": [{"headline", "score", "importance", "rank",
                 "rank_of", "status", "paragraphs": [...], "source", "url"}]}],
 "extra_sections": [{"title": "The Bigger Picture — Daily AI Analysis", "paragraphs": [...]},
                    {"title": "About this edition", "paragraphs": [...]}]}
Any other key is ignored and never printed. _full_report_reference_input.json is a
complete real example.

9. REBUILD
====================================================================
1. Take build_pdf.py from Bulletin blueprint V1 / Permanent Assets.
2. Build the input JSON from the day's verified entries (sections 4, 5, 8).
3. Run: python build_pdf.py --input report_input.json --kind report --out Full_Report_D-M-YY.pdf
4. The program installs ReportLab if missing, renders until "Page x of y" and the
   contents page numbers are stable, and checks that every font is embedded.
5. Test after any change to the program: build from _full_report_reference_input.json
   and compare page by page with _full_report_reference_file.pdf (38 pages); do the
   same for the bulletin reference.

11. FINAL CHECK BEFORE SHIPPING
====================================================================
1. The cutoff and the count are stated; held-back items are in the QA record.
2. Categories in order, highest score first inside each, Fun Side last.
3. Every story has a publisher name and a working direct link.
4. Every story keeps its hedges and attribution; headline-only stories carry the note.
5. The Bigger Picture labels every claim and cites item numbers.
6. Page count, contents page numbers and "Page x of y" agree; every font is embedded.
7. Spot-check the first page and two story pages against the reference PDF.
8. Publication needs Rafael's explicit approval.
