## THE BIGGER PICTURE — DAILY AI ANALYSIS

*What today's developments could mean for technology, business and everyday life*

### 1. What AI can do now

Fact: Meta says in a post on its research blog that mathematicians working with its Muse Spark chatbot answered five questions that were still open, with the chatbot suggesting proofs and examples that the mathematicians checked and corrected; Meta adds that other teams independently announced answers to some of the same problems (item 71). The claims come from Meta, and no outside review is reported.

Fact: New Scientist reports that Lean, the program mathematicians use to confirm that AI-found proofs are correct, was fooled in July when an engineer used AI to find and exploit two bugs in two of its checking engines to back a false claim of disproving a famous problem; Lean's developers say the next version will ship with four checking engines instead of one, though the remaining risk is smaller, not zero (item 72).

Fact: HIT Consultant reports that AKASA says its new AI turns hospital inpatient charts into billing codes in under 90 seconds, a job it says takes human coders 30 to 60 minutes, and that blinded third-party tests showed it matched or beat experienced coders; those results come from the company (item 90). 2 Minute Medicine reports on a study of 11 hospitals where deaths among high-risk patients were 18.6% after an AI warning score began alerting rapid response teams, down from 23.1% before; it was a before-and-after comparison, not a randomised trial, and only 46.6% of stays produced an alert (item 91).

Fact: The Verge reports that Meta has opened up the code of its Muse assistant so people can connect it to their own screens and sensors, and that Meta made only 5,000 of its own home-control box (item 60). Breaking Defense reports, from an Army memo, that the US Army will set up a Futures and Autonomous Systems Command and a new office for buying autonomous systems, and wants autonomous weapons, combat vehicles and supply boats in service by the 2028 budget year (item 52).

Inference: the pattern across these items is that AI is producing results faster than the tools that check them. The mathematicians checked Meta's proofs, and the checker itself was tricked once. The hospital study and the billing claim both come with the caveat that the numbers are the sellers' or a single comparison. The question for the next weeks is less "can it do this?" and more "who verifies it, and how fast?".

### 2. People and society

Fact: Startup Fortune, citing CNBC, reports that US employers added 29,000 jobs in September against about 84,000 expected, that unemployment rose to 4.2% against a forecast of 4.1%, and that revisions took 60,000 jobs off earlier months. The outlet ties this to a growing number of companies, such as BMW, that name AI as a reason for cuts, but the jobs data does not show that AI caused the slowdown (item 96).

Fact: citybiz, citing a Partnership for New York City report, says AI companies rented more than 2.2 million square feet of New York office space in the first half of 2026, more than double their total for all of 2025, while entry-level job postings since 2022 fell by 23% to 41% in fields such as finance, administration and writing; the report stops short of blaming AI alone (item 98).

Fact: The Wall Street Journal reports that some middle-school students are turning to AI chatbots as friends in place of real people (item 95), and the Financial Times reports that students accused of misusing AI are hiring lawyers to fight the accusations (item 99). Both are known to us from their headlines only. TechCrunch reports that Pope Leo XIV wrote on X on 2 October that "it is becoming urgent to distinguish human art from what machines produce" (item 94).

Inference: the human side of AI today is about trust and entry points: young workers fear the first rung of the career ladder is thinner, students and schools are arguing over what counts as their own work, and a major religious voice has taken the artists' side. Weak signal: a weak jobs report and a drop in entry-level postings together may feed a political story that AI takes jobs, even where the data does not yet prove it. Unknown: how many of the missing jobs, if any, are caused by AI rather than other pressures.

### 3. Safety, security and law

L'Unione Sarda, citing Corriere della Sera, reports that a former president of the Italian bank Fideuram was tricked in February by a fake WhatsApp message and a call using an AI-copied voice into sending about 95 million euros, of which at least 36 million is still missing; this is a press report, not a court finding (item 29).

Fact: TechCrunch reports that Apple will add controls to the Mac setting that lets an app reach all a user's files and messages, because AI agents make that access riskier (item 30).
