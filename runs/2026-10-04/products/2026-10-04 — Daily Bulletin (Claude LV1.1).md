**6 MEDIUM — Researchers screened a bacterium's whole set of protein pairings by feeding AlphaFold3 pools of proteins instead of one pair at a time.** (report item 59)  
Scienmag reports, in a text carried by Bioengineer.org, that Todor and colleagues, writing in Molecular Systems Biology, screened all 113,050 possible protein pairs in the bacterium Mycoplasma genitalium with AlphaFold3, Google DeepMind's protein structure model, by submitting random pools of 10 to 25 proteins as single jobs rather than every pair separately. The pooling cut the number of AlphaFold jobs 100-fold, roughly halved the runtime and, for reasons the authors cannot yet explain, found true interactions more accurately, though nearly 18,000 pairs involving species-specific proteins could not be evaluated at all. *Scienmag, REPORTED*

## HEALTH — 3
**6 MEDIUM — The American Academy of Pediatrics told pediatricians how generative AI should and should not be used on children.** (report item 65)  
The American Academy of Pediatrics said on 3 Oct 2026 that it has published a policy statement, out in the November 2026 issue of Pediatrics, on using generative AI in children's clinical care, calling for tools built for children rather than adapted from adult models, strong privacy safeguards, bias checks and "rigorous human oversight and accountability", in the words of lead author Srinivasan Suresh. The group notes that most such tools are trained on data in which children are underrepresented and few have been properly tested in pediatrics; the statement covers clinicians' use only, with separate guidance for families still in preparation. *American Academy of Pediatrics, CONFIRMED*

**6 MEDIUM — Mayo Clinic researchers say an AI model spotted pancreatic cancer risk in routine health records up to five years early.** (report item 66)  
Medical News Today reports that Mayo Clinic researchers presented, at the American College of Surgeons Clinical Congress 2026, an AI model trained on the records of almost 40,000 patients that distinguished future pancreatic cancer cases from controls with an AUROC of 0.853 five years before diagnosis, a score where 0.5 is chance and 1.0 is perfect. The findings are not peer reviewed and do not yet show better outcomes in practice; lead author Chris Varghese said the model's signals came largely from routine blood counts, and the team is now running prospective tests and plans to try it at a non-Mayo health system. *Medical News Today, PRELIMINARY*
