## THE BIGGER PICTURE — DAILY AI ANALYSIS

*What today's developments could mean for technology, business and everyday life*

### 1. What AI can do now

Fact: The Verge reports that OpenAI released 722 manuscripts of mathematics results, grouped into 372 families, from an AI model it has not released; the independent Advisory Group on Mathematics and AI says they include solutions to "hundreds" of open questions, and OpenAI says the average result took about three hours of ChatGPT Pro thinking (item 125). Fact: TechCrunch reports that the Paris-based developer Mistral AI released Mistral Large 4, a one-trillion-parameter model, for now only through a guarded public endpoint; Mistral says the open weights (the files that let others run the model) follow in about three weeks after safety testing, its claim to beat some Chinese models is its own, and benchmark results are still pending (item 94). Fact: Nikkei Asia reports that Chinese AI companies including DeepSeek and Xiaomi released 16 models last month (item 103). Inference: the most capable systems are still announced before outsiders can test them, so claims run ahead of checks.

Fact: 9to5Google reports that Anthropic's Claude add-on for Google Docs, Sheets and Slides is in public beta for paid Claude users, with an "ask before edits" mode (item 95). Fact: TechCrunch reports that the start-up Hark released Hark Pro to everyone, an assistant that carries out tasks on the user's own computer; Hark says it will not sell ads or data, though users must connect their email, calendar and payment cards (item 96). Fact: SiliconANGLE, citing interviews given to CNBC, reports that Meta, Walmart, Stripe and Sierra are building the Personal Agent Protocol so businesses can tell authorised AI agents apart; Sierra's Bret Taylor says there "will be chaos until such a standard exists" (item 97). Fact: GitHub says Git activity on its service grew from 218.2 billion to 473.3 billion events a month in a year as AI agents work alongside people, and it is rebuilding its storage to cope (item 100). Fact: ClickOnDetroit reports that Waymo began fully driverless driving in Detroit, with rides limited to its employees for now (item 84). Inference: AI agents are moving from demonstrations into everyday tools, and the plumbing around them (standards, storage, permissions) is being rebuilt only now.

### 2. People and society

Fact: Radio Ink, citing Pew Research Center, reports that 71% of Americans expect AI to mean fewer jobs over the next 20 years, up from 64% in August 2024 (item 162). Fact: Brookings researchers, studying about 25,000 Danish workers, found no effect of chatbot use on pay, hours or hiring up to December 2024, but found work being reorganised, with new tasks such as checking AI output (item 163). Fact: Microsoft published an essay by the Nobel-winning economist Daron Acemoglu predicting that AI will raise economic output by only about 1.5% over ten years and replace at most 5% of jobs, The Decoder reports; it is his forecast, not a measured result (item 38).

Fact: Reuters reports that FICO, the credit-score company, is cutting about 15% of its workforce in a restructuring it links to AI (item 15). Fact: the Financial Times reports, citing people familiar with the plans, that HSBC wants to cut about half of management and specialist roles in its UK wealth business, while HSBC's own statement gives no figures (item 29). Fact: Reuters reports that Norway's DNB will lay off about 400 people as it adopts AI agents (item 30). Fact: CBS News reports that HubSpot is laying off about 660 workers, and that its chief executive wrote the decision "is not driven by AI-related efficiencies" (item 14). Inference: companies now name AI in their cuts more openly than the measured data shows effects so far; the surveys and studies look backwards, while the cuts are being decided now.

Fact: MeriTalk reports that Senators Josh Hawley and Chris Murphy proposed a bill making operators and developers of AI agents liable for hacking damage; it is only a proposal, and President Trump has repeatedly rejected extra AI regulation (item 144).
