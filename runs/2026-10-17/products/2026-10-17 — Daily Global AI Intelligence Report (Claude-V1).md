## THE BIGGER PICTURE — DAILY AI ANALYSIS

*What today's developments could mean for technology, business and everyday life*

### What is happening

The AI build is now a fight over the price of money, not over demand for AI. Demand holds: Amazon Web Services says its AI servers pay back in under three years (item 21). Money is the problem: the 10-year US Treasury yield has sat above 5% since mid-September, partly on the tech giants' borrowing, CFO Dive notes (item 9), and the cost of insuring the $50 billion of debt of CoreWeave, which rents out AI computing power, hit a record, Reuters reports (item 11). Kevin Warsh, chair of the Federal Reserve, says AI hits demand first and supply later, and the ride will be bumpy (item 9). Governments draw lines too: China's draft export bans and limits on 23 AI technologies (item 1).

### Conclusions

Our view: this corrects the cost of money for AI, not yet the demand for it, and sorts companies by balance sheet. Cash-rich giants keep building; debt-funded renters face bond yields that briefly passed 16% (item 11) and go first into mergers, sales or restructurings; even Anthropic offers lenders more than 10%, the Financial Times reports (item 14); makers of scarce chips, memory and power keep their pricing.

### Key points

1. Weeks ahead: the giants keep spending. Meta and Tesla report on 21 October and Microsoft on 28 October, by their own notices. Chance that Amazon, Alphabet, Microsoft and Meta all hold or raise their 2027 spending plans: about 65%. Why: Goldman Sachs expects five giant AI cloud companies to spend about $1.2 trillion next year (item 15), and Morgan Stanley says the four cover their interest about 30 times over (item 25). Confirms: 2027 plans held or raised. Weakens: one of them cutting 2027.

2. Three months: the weakest AI borrowers get sorted out. Chance that at least one AI cloud renter announces a merger, sale or restructuring by mid-January: about 50%. Why: CoreWeave's shares lost nearly a quarter in a week (item 11), and the research firm Gartner expects more than 80% of such renters to stop operating independently by 2030 (item 21); though Evercore says the model still works (item 11). Confirms: a deal or a missed payment. Weakens: CoreWeave borrowing at a normal cost again.

3. To 9 December: borrowing stays expensive. Chance the Federal Reserve holds on 28 October: about 75%; raises at least once by its 8 to 9 December meeting: about 55%. Why: the 10-year yield is above 5% (item 9); hot inflation data raised bets on a rise, and Barclays expects quarter-point rises in December and January, Reuters reports (item 10) (item 15). Confirms: a Fed hint of a rise, or hot inflation in mid-November. Weakens: cooling inflation or yields back under 5%.

4. Six months: the AI market splits in two. Chance that China's draft becomes a final rule covering AI models within six months: about 50%. Why: two ministries back it, citing national security (item 1). The stake: four of the top five on one widely used model ranking were Chinese last week, and poorer countries pick them on cost (item 63); in our reading, a ban on exporting China's biggest future models would push those buyers toward US and European open models such as Beam, from the US start-up Reflection AI (item 58). Confirms: a final list. Weakens: an exception for published models.

5. One to three years: some of the build is written off. Chance that a large listed AI company writes off $10 billion or more of AI assets by 2028: about 55%. Why: Morgan Stanley puts AI computing spending at about $1.58 trillion in 2027, the year of the largest cash shortfall (item 25), and Gartner says more than $3.5 trillion of AI infrastructure spending could lose value by 2030 (item 21). Confirms: a first big write-off in 2027. Weakens: AI revenue growing faster than debt.
