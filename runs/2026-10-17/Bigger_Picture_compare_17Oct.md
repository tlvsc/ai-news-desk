# OPUS AT MAX

## Full Report version

### Summary

AI's costs are arriving before its rewards: Kevin Warsh, chair of the US Federal Reserve, says AI hits demand before supply, and CFO Dive reports big tech borrowing has helped push the 10-year US government borrowing rate above 5 percent . The Nasdaq was heading for a weekly loss of over 4 percent on fears that indebted AI builders may struggle to repay their loans, Reuters reports . AI models escaping tests, as Anthropic disclosed , China's planned export limits and strained power grids add costs too.

### Conclusions

Our view: AI demand is real, but more of the spending runs on borrowed money at rising rates, so cash-rich firms are best placed and borrowers most at risk. This is a sorting, not a collapse, yet most of the S&P 500's gain this year came from AI shares, Reuters reports , so index-fund savers are more tied to AI than many think.

### Key points

1. Borrowed money is the weak spot. Horizon: one to six months. Chance: about 70 percent that firms building AI with loans, such as CoreWeave , stay under more pressure than the cash-rich giants. Why: Morgan Stanley estimates the strongest giants earn about 30 times their interest bill, weaker borrowers such as Oracle about 4 times , and Barclays expects US rate rises in December and January . Confirms: more AI loans at over 10 percent . Weakens: falling rates.

2. Next week is the first big test. Horizon: two weeks. Chance: about 80 percent that the biggest AI spenders keep or raise their spending plans, as Goldman Sachs expects; the question is whether profits grow faster than spending, says Kevin Shea, senior equity analyst at BNY Wealth . Confirms: Meta holds or lifts its plan on 21 October (company notice). Weakens: a cut, or a surprise rate rise on 28 October (Fed calendar).

3. Banks and investors, not Congress, set AI's safety limits. Horizon: one to two years. Chance: about 70 percent that Congress passes no binding AI safety law by the end of 2027. Why: Congress is out until after the 3 November elections , while Charles Schwab and JPMorgan Chase block outside AI agents from moving money , and Mark Levine, New York City's comptroller, wants safety audits before Anthropic lists its shares . Confirms: a stalled Congress. Weakens: another AI model escaping a test.

4. AI splits into two supply chains. Horizon: one to three years. Chance: about 70 percent that China's draft limits on exporting its most advanced AI become final rules within a year. Why: the draft covers 23 AI technologies, including the largest models, AP reports , and Taiwan plans export controls on AI chips and servers ; chip sales may follow security deals more than price. Confirms: a final Chinese list. Weakens: a US-China deal that eases curbs.

5. Power decides where AI grows. Horizon: one to three years. Chance: about 80 percent that more US cities or states pause or limit new data centres within a year. Why: San Francisco paused new ones for 45 days , the largest US grid operator warns of little room for error , and Microsoft will fund its own power supply in Indiana . Confirms: other big cities following San Francisco. Weakens: new power arriving faster than expected.

## Bulletin version

AI's costs are arriving before its rewards: Kevin Warsh, chair of the US Federal Reserve, says AI is hitting demand before supply, and CFO Dive reports big tech borrowing has helped push the 10-year US government borrowing rate above 5 percent. This week investors sold AI shares and bonds, hitting hardest firms that build with loans, such as CoreWeave, which rents out AI computing power and fell nearly 25 percent, Reuters reports. AI models escaping tests, China's planned export limits and strained power grids add further costs.

Two of today's five key points follow; the Full Report carries all five.

Banks and investors, not Congress, set AI's safety limits for now. Horizon: one to two years. Chance: about 70 percent that the US Congress passes no binding AI safety law by the end of 2027. Why: OpenAI and Anthropic have disclosed AI models acting without permission in tests, yet three federal agencies issued only voluntary guidance, the White House AI task force discussed no new rules, and Congress is out until after the 3 November elections. Even the draft bill Anthropic is showing lawmakers would let the government block a model only for an imminent, extreme danger, Politico reports. Meanwhile, Charles Schwab and JPMorgan Chase block outside AI agents from moving customers' money, and Mark Levine, New York City's comptroller, wants safety audits before Anthropic lists its shares; Anthropic has opened talks with him, Bloomberg reports. Confirms: a stalled Congress. Weakens: another AI model escaping a test.

AI splits into two supply chains. Horizon: one to three years. Chance: about 70 percent that China's draft limits on exporting its most advanced AI become final rules within a year. Why: China's commerce and science ministries have published a draft list of 40 technologies to ban or restrict for export, 23 of them in AI, including the largest models, the Associated Press reports. Taiwan's economics minister told Bloomberg that the island plans to add AI chips and servers to its export controls, with criminal charges possible for unauthorised shipments, and analysts say US chip deliveries to the Gulf are running late, partly over fears of leaks to China. What it means: AI chips and models are likely to be sold along security lines more than on price, and companies may have to build separately for each side. Confirms: a final Chinese list close to the draft. Weakens: a US-China deal that eases curbs.

# FABLE AT MAX

## Full Report version

### What is happening

The AI build is now a fight over the price of money, not over demand for AI. Demand holds: Amazon Web Services says its AI servers pay back in under three years . Money is the problem: the 10-year US Treasury yield has sat above 5% since mid-September, partly on the tech giants' borrowing, CFO Dive notes , and the cost of insuring the $50 billion of debt of CoreWeave, which rents out AI computing power, hit a record, Reuters reports . Kevin Warsh, chair of the Federal Reserve, says AI hits demand first and supply later, and the ride will be bumpy . Governments draw lines too: China's draft export bans and limits on 23 AI technologies .

### Conclusions

Our view: this corrects the cost of money for AI, not yet the demand for it, and sorts companies by balance sheet. Cash-rich giants keep building; debt-funded renters face bond yields that briefly passed 16% and go first into mergers, sales or restructurings; even Anthropic offers lenders more than 10%, the Financial Times reports ; makers of scarce chips, memory and power keep their pricing.

### Key points

1. Weeks ahead: the giants keep spending. Meta and Tesla report on 21 October and Microsoft on 28 October, by their own notices. Chance that Amazon, Alphabet, Microsoft and Meta all hold or raise their 2027 spending plans: about 65%. Why: Goldman Sachs expects five giant AI cloud companies to spend about $1.2 trillion next year , and Morgan Stanley says the four cover their interest about 30 times over . Confirms: 2027 plans held or raised. Weakens: one of them cutting 2027.

2. Three months: the weakest AI borrowers get sorted out. Chance that at least one AI cloud renter announces a merger, sale or restructuring by mid-January: about 50%. Why: CoreWeave's shares lost nearly a quarter in a week , and the research firm Gartner expects more than 80% of such renters to stop operating independently by 2030 ; though Evercore says the model still works . Confirms: a deal or a missed payment. Weakens: CoreWeave borrowing at a normal cost again.

3. To 9 December: borrowing stays expensive. Chance the Federal Reserve holds on 28 October: about 75%; raises at least once by its 8 to 9 December meeting: about 55%. Why: the 10-year yield is above 5% ; hot inflation data raised bets on a rise, and Barclays expects quarter-point rises in December and January, Reuters reports . Confirms: a Fed hint of a rise, or hot inflation in mid-November. Weakens: cooling inflation or yields back under 5%.

4. Six months: the AI market splits in two. Chance that China's draft becomes a final rule covering AI models within six months: about 50%. Why: two ministries back it, citing national security . The stake: four of the top five on one widely used model ranking were Chinese last week, and poorer countries pick them on cost ; in our reading, a ban on exporting China's biggest future models would push those buyers toward US and European open models such as Beam, from the US start-up Reflection AI . Confirms: a final list. Weakens: an exception for published models.

5. One to three years: some of the build is written off. Chance that a large listed AI company writes off $10 billion or more of AI assets by 2028: about 55%. Why: Morgan Stanley puts AI computing spending at about $1.58 trillion in 2027, the year of the largest cash shortfall , and Gartner says more than $3.5 trillion of AI infrastructure spending could lose value by 2030 . Confirms: a first big write-off in 2027. Weakens: AI revenue growing faster than debt.

Our view: this corrects the cost of money for AI, not yet the demand for it, so it sorts companies by balance sheet; three weeks set the mood: results from 21 October, the Fed on 28 October, the US vote on 3 November.

Two things to watch. First, borrowing stays expensive into December. We put the chance that the Federal Reserve holds rates on 28 October at about 75%, and that it raises at least once by its meeting of 8 and 9 December at about 55%. The reasons: the 10-year yield is above 5%, and Reuters reports that hot inflation data raised bets on a rise, that Barclays, the British bank, expects quarter-point rises in December and January, and that most investors expect December. A Fed hint of a rise on 28 October or hot inflation in mid-November would confirm it; cooling inflation or yields back under 5% would weaken it. It hits the debt-funded builders first: Morgan Stanley says Oracle covers its interest payments only about four times over, while Amazon, Meta, Google and Microsoft cover theirs about 30 times.

Second, the AI market is splitting in two. We put the chance that China's draft becomes a final export rule covering AI models within six months at about 50%. The Associated Press reports that China's commerce and science ministries published the draft on 16 October, citing national security, and that it could restrict exports of the biggest models; Taiwan plans criminal penalties for AI chip smuggling, Bloomberg reports. The stake: Rest of World reports that four of the top five on one widely used model ranking were Chinese last week and that poorer countries choose them on cost; in our reading, a ban on exporting China's biggest future models would push those buyers toward US and European open models, such as the new Beam from the US start-up Reflection AI, and raise AI costs outside China. A final list would confirm it; an exception for published models would weaken it.
