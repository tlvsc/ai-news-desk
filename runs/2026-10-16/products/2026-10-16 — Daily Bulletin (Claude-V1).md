## RESEARCH AND SCIENCE — 2
**8 HIGH — Google's quantum team has shown on real hardware a way to make the special quantum building blocks that error-corrected computers need with far less error and far fewer qubits.** (report item 70)  
Quantum Computing Report reports that Google Quantum AI, in a study published in Science, ran a technique called magic state cultivation on its Willow superconducting chip, cutting errors about 40-fold and reaching a record 99.99% fidelity (a measure of how accurate a quantum state is) for the special "T-states" that universal quantum computers require. Until now such states were expected to take up 90% to 95% of a fault-tolerant machine's qubits when made by the older distillation method, while cultivation uses a small error-checking code and then moves the cleaned state into a larger one. *Quantum Computing Report, CONFIRMED*

The authors say the agents still lack the "taste" to choose good problems, and warn that rising compute costs could make research budgets matter more, even in theoretical physics. *The Decoder, REPORTED*
