17 SEPTEMBER 2026 — RAFAEL APPROVED CONTINUOUS-CLIP GENERATION AND STITCHING RULES
Applies to Headlines video in V1, V1.1 and V2 and to every blueprint version that is reused. This dated instruction overrides conflicting older trimming, freeze and crossfade recipes. Historical edition outputs and source clips remain unchanged.

1. Desired result: one natural continuous presenter performance. Plan the joins before generation, not after a completed cut film.
2. Use one approved canonical boundary image: same presenter, framing, camera, lighting, hands resting in the agreed exact position, mouth closed, TV logo state and matching reflections. Use the identical image for first and last frame conditioning WHERE THE ACTUAL ENGINE SUPPORTS IT. Reference-image prompts alone are not endpoint constraints. Verify node/model support and a short result before claiming it works; do not invent JSON fields or silently change engine, wiring, seed, voice or model.
3. Per-clip continuity (hold precision clarified by Rafael, 19 September): begin already settled with both hands resting together on the table, mouth closed, face straight toward the camera and eyes directly into the lens, with the approved logo and reflection. Start the story graphic at about second 2; finish narration and gestures, return naturally to the identical logo state, and end in the same settled hands-on-table, face-to-camera pose. The exact opening and ending hold lengths are flexible. Keep the established speech-duration formula unchanged; do not add extra seconds or accelerate speech to enforce a precise hold.
4. Write those stages and duration explicitly in each clip prompt and all mirrored JSON metadata. A requested second count is not proof the generator obeyed it. Check the rendered first/last frames, hands, face, mouth, framing, logo, TV colour, reflections, audio and lip sync before acceptance. Reject or repair only the affected failed clip within the authorized run; never loop identical failed generations.
5. For dependable screen timing, the agreed future-production method may use a consistent logo-screen presenter plate plus a controlled story graphic and its correctly matched reflection. Preserve the approved curved TV geometry, bezel, lighting and presenter occlusion. This is a planned production method, not permission to repaint a satisfactory TV during a stitching repair. Do not improvise a new screen, border, colour treatment or reflection.
6. First stitch the complete original clips and inspect the joins. Add subtitles and source credits only after picture continuity works. Preserve every original frame by default; cut frames only when necessary and evidenced, never automatically trim to the last spoken word or introduce internal speech cuts as a routine. Preserve full words, breaths, gestures and settling movements. Verify source and export frame counts.
7. Use direct last-to-first joins when they really match. If a presenter-only transition is needed, confine it to the presenter; leave TV/background/reflection pixels unchanged outside the actual foreground matte. Never run whole-frame morphing, optical flow or crossfades as a shortcut that distorts the TV. No unrelated logo bridge across the presenter. Do not certify a hard join as fluent just because it is one MP4.
8. Validate real-time boundary playback, full-file decode, audio continuity/lip sync, screen/reflection stability, captions after picture lock, and delivery readback/hash. State any review limits honestly. A rules update is not evidence that endpoint conditioning has been implemented or that today's film is visually accepted.
9. Preserve original assets and prior versions; no deletions. Today's authorized repair starts from the complete September 17 source clips and preserves their TV/reflections; future logo timing is not applied retroactively to today's TV.
