THE RULES (read them first, in full): WORKDIR/rules/Headlines_Master_Rules_Structure.txt Sections A, B, G and the 15 September 2026 section. They win over any example here.
Script shape for a story: "<a short natural spoken introduction>. <the deed in one plain sentence>. <one plain sentence: what it means or the honest doubt>." The introduction suits the subject and briefs the item like a newsreader (Rule B2a), for example "In politics", "From Washington", "On the markets", "Turning to safety", "On the road". INTRODUCTIONS ROTATE (Rule G2): never use an introduction the previous day used. The previous day's introductions: {PREV_LEADINS}. The fun clip opens with a short lighter introduction that is not the previous day's either. The teaser starts with "Also in the full report." (a full stop, never a colon) and gives two items from teaser_items, each a short full sentence. The Bigger Picture starts with "And for the bigger picture:" and says what moves the market and one thing to watch with why it matters, in plain words, from WORKDIR/bigger_picture_bulletin.json.
LEVEL 5, THE SHAPE (Rafi, 5 Oct 2026, after a set he called long and complicated): every spoken sentence at most 14 words. Sentence one says who did what, subject first, in the plainest words ("Hackers posing as an-thropic staff tried to break into A I experts' accounts."). Never open with a side clause ("Posing as A I firm an-thropic, hackers sent ..." is wrong), never stack two descriptions on one name, at most one comma. The last sentence says WHY IT MATTERS TO ORDINARY PEOPLE, taken from the entry's importance_line ("That could make A I and phone chips cost more."); what matters only to the company is not a why ("It is Schneider's biggest deal ever" is wrong). When a line is too long, cut words; never add a clause to explain.
COMPANY TAGS (Rule G1.2): a company or organisation worth under one hundred billion dollars gets a two to three word tag ("Japanese engineering group Kawasaki"); if unsure of its size, tag it. The robotics clip says what the robot or vehicle does (Rule B2).
SYLLABLE BUDGET (speaking time = syllables / 4.4 per second, rounded up to the half second): a story 35 to 40 syllables (8 to 9 s, aim 8); fun and teaser about 35 (8 s); the Bigger Picture at most 48 (11 s). Never more than 53 (12 s). Do not speed the presenter up; cut words instead.
Numbers are spoken as words ("seventy three percent"); write no digits. Acronyms are spaced so they are spoken letter by letter ("A I", "C E O"). Pronunciation cue: write Anthropic as "an-thropic" and Nvidia as "N-vidia" and Kawasaki as "Kawa saki" only where spoken. Never speak an outlet name. Keep every hedge.
"screen": one line for the glowing blue holographic screen behind the presenter: a small simple symbol that matches the story, text free and number free, for example "a small glowing laptop with a closed padlock on its screen, simple text-free symbol". Easy for a video model to draw; no people, no faces, no logos.
